H.R. 1 expands work requirements for SNAP and Medicaid. The Workforce for the Community Act introduces new community engagement verification requirements. States face financial penalties for SNAP payment error rates above 6%.

The tools states have today were not built for this moment. Quarterly wage databases are months behind. Commercial verification services charge per query with prices that go up every year. And when the data is wrong or incomplete, caseworkers absorb the cost in follow-up calls, manual processing, and the ever increasing backlog. Meanwhile, applicants face longer waits and higher burdens.

Real-time payroll data is part of the answer, but raw data is not verification. A payroll connection can pull income records from an employer's system. It cannot tell you whether those records contain what a caseworker needs to make an eligibility determination. Without a layer that validates, contextualizes, and delivers that data in a usable format, states trade one set of problems for another.

VMI addresses all of these pressures: real-time income data that reduces payment errors, a validation layer that ensures caseworkers receive only eligibility-grade reports, support for work and community engagement requirements, and a streamlined process that removes the verification bottleneck for caseworkers and applicants.

The process

How VMI works

VMI handles the entire data journey from the applicant's payroll provider, through a consent-driven workflow, to a caseworker-ready report. States get a complete service, not just a data feed.

Step 01

Applicant receives a secure link

During or after applying for benefits, the applicant is told they need to verify earned income and is shown a link to verify their income.

Step 02

Applicant consents and connects their payroll

The applicant reviews a plain-language consent screen, then connects to their payroll provider. No pay stubs to find. No documents to upload.

Step 03

Verified income data is delivered to the caseworker

A standardized income report, verified directly from the payroll source, is delivered to the state benefit system. The report passes through validation before delivery so the caseworker only receives eligibility-grade data and can act immediately.

Service design

We do not just deliver data. We fix the process.

Most verification vendors hand off data and walk away. DPW embeds with your team to find and fix the problems that no data feed can solve.

01

Finding

A form question that created unnecessary work

In one state, the application asked for a specific number of hours worked rather than a range. When paystubs showed normal week-to-week variation (39, 37, 42, 40 hours), caseworkers were forced to issue Requests for Information to verify the client's expected working hours even though the paystubs were valid proof of income. DPW identified the problem through service design analysis and recommended changing the question to ask for a range, eliminating unnecessary RFIs caused by a form-wording problem that had nothing to do with data access.

02

Finding

40% of applicants did not know they had a next step

In another state, approximately 40% of people completing SNAP renewals were not submitting required income documents because they did not realize they needed to. The required actions were buried deep in a generic PDF. DPW worked with the state to add a clear alert on the post-submission landing page, making the required next steps visible for the first time. This workflow improvement led to a 35% increase in income document submissions and saw an increase across all verification methods, not just VMI.

03

Finding

Caseworkers were over-verifying without realizing it

DPW's case review analysis found that caseworkers were frequently requesting household composition verification at unnecessary steps in the benefit lifecycle. After DPW flagged this pattern to agency leadership, a policy bulletin was published clarifying when such requests were appropriate. This reduced unnecessary burden on both applicants and caseworkers.

Every state engagement includes a discovery sprint with interviews of policy experts, caseworkers, quality control workers, community-based organizations, and applicants. These are not one-time exercises. DPW conducts ongoing case reviews, feedback analysis, and service design improvements throughout the partnership.

Our model

A better model for income verification

Traditional verification vendors lock you into rising costs and proprietary systems. When you need to switch, you start from scratch.

DPW is different. We are a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit. We are legally prohibited from profiting on this work. Your price reflects our operating costs and nothing more. As more states join the platform, the per-state cost decreases. States share infrastructure instead of building alone.

Our code is open source under the AGPL-3.0 license, published on GitHub. If you adopt VMI, you have full access to the source code, the integration architecture, and all documentation. No vendor lock-in. No black boxes. No surprise overages.
